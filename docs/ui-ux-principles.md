# UI/UX Principles

## Product character
Construction Management is an operational enterprise application. Clarity, traceability, information density and fast task completion take priority over decoration.

## Principles
1. **Clarity over decoration** — every screen must make location, state and next actions obvious.
2. **Exception first** — overdue, failed, blocked and pending work is surfaced before passive totals.
3. **Consistent semantics** — success, warning, danger, information and neutral states have stable meanings.
4. **Dense but readable** — use tables for registers, cards for summaries, charts for trends and timelines for history.
5. **Progressive disclosure** — complex records expose essential information first and secondary detail on demand.
6. **Traceability** — related drawings, RFIs, inspections, NCRs, activities and actions should be navigable relationships.
7. **Accessible by default** — keyboard access, visible focus, semantic controls, contrast and non-color status cues are required.
8. **Responsive by task** — desktop favors management and registers; tablet/mobile prioritizes site workflows.
9. **Actionable states** — loading, empty and error states explain what is happening and what the user can do next.
10. **Stable patterns** — detail pages, forms, tables, filters and destructive actions should behave consistently across modules.

## Visual system
White is the dominant workspace surface. A restrained blue is used for primary interaction. Green means successful/approved/completed, amber means pending/attention, red means failed/critical/overdue, blue means informational and neutral tones represent drafts/inactive states.

Typography uses the system/Inter stack with compact enterprise sizing. Spacing follows a 4px base scale. Focus indicators must remain visible.

## Component rules
Use dialogs for confirmations and short actions, drawers for contextual previews/filters/history, and full pages for complex construction workflows. Avoid modal-heavy workflows.

## Data presentation
Registers should support search, filtering, sorting and pagination as their modules mature. Status must never rely on color alone. Dashboards should emphasize exceptions and trends rather than collections of equally weighted KPI cards.
